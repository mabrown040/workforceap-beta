## Why this matters
This call was not just a website copy review. It surfaced a fuller product thesis:

- WorkforceAP is being treated as a full workforce operating system
- Members should remain free
- Partners and employers are the clearest monetization paths
- The public site, portals, employer workflow, partner accountability, and docs/sales materials all need to work together

## Office-hours style summary

### What problem the team is actually trying to solve
WorkforceAP is trying to help disadvantaged members move from interest to training to placement, while also giving employers, partners, and admins one integrated system to manage that journey.

### Who the real users are
- Members / students
- Parents and families
- Employers hiring talent
- Referral/community partners
- Internal admins / counselors / super admins
- Board and strategic partners

### Strongest signs of real demand in the call
- Mike repeatedly framed this as immediately useful for real partner/org targets, not hypothetical users
- Specific partner examples came up: Goodwill, Workforce Solutions, Gary Jobcore, churches, nonprofits, DIR companies
- The team discussed concrete monetization and workflow ownership, not just visuals
- The employer matching / approval workflow triggered the strongest excitement because it maps directly to placement and revenue

### Current status quo / what WorkforceAP replaces
The call implies WorkforceAP is replacing a messy status quo of:
- fragmented training + placement + partner tracking
- manual counselor workflows
- disconnected course progress tracking
- employer job collection + review done manually
- no shared partner accountability layer
- weak public explanation of the full model

### Narrowest wedge revealed by the call
The clearest near-term wedge is:

**A workforce advancement platform that keeps members free while helping WorkforceAP and partner/employer relationships run through one system for applications, training progress, job workflows, and placement support.**

Within that, the sharpest near-term commercial wedge is probably:

**Employer + partner workflows that generate better placements and clearer accountability.**

## Key product premises surfaced by the call
1. Members should not be charged.
2. Partners and employers can be charged if the value is clear.
3. The public site should drive belief and clarity, not just describe the org.
4. Portal naming consistency matters because it signals product maturity.
5. Employer job ingestion and candidate matching are strategic differentiators.
6. Partner reporting / accountability is central to long-term value.
7. Documentation and one-pagers are required for sales/board/partner adoption.
8. Austin is the launch context, not the long-term ceiling.
